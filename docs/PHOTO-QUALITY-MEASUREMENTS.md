# Photo quality measurements (T-265, WP-10 step 1)

Generated by `npm run photo-quality` (scripts/photo-quality-measure.ts). Offline: sharp (libwebp/libjpeg), no browser, no network. Deterministic: two runs give a byte-identical file. Do not edit by hand.

Where this is used: the adopted qualities and caps are in `packages/shared/src/photo-quality.ts` and described in `docs/ADMIN.md` ("Photo quality (WP-10)"). The final rerun on the photographer's originals (`samples/originals/`, gitignored) is VERIFY row 53 in `docs/VERIFY.md`; until then the numbers below are provisional.

## Method

- Samples: 24 photos (0 from samples/originals, 24 legacy gallery JPEGs, every n-th file of legacy/fotky_web/galleries in sorted order; the legacy files are already lossy web JPEGs of about 1 MB, not camera originals).
- Library versions: sharp 0.35.5, libvips 8.18.7, libwebp 1.6.0.
- Candidate = canvas-like downscale (halving steps, bilinear, last step at most 2x) then optional unsharp mask on luma (sigma 0.6 px, amount per tier from plan 3.2), then encode. Reference = one direct Lanczos3 resize of the same source to the same size. Quality = luma SSIM (8x8 windows, step 4) of the decoded candidate against that reference. Bytes are exact encoded sizes; KB = 1024 B.
- sharp quality q*100 stands in for canvas.toBlob(quality): same libwebp / libjpeg family, but the browser encoder settings differ a little. Whether the real admin encoder matches is checked by `npm run admin-encode-check -- --report` (browser run, see below).
- p90 = nearest-rank 90th percentile over the samples. "Plan target met" compares the p90 bytes with the cap of plan 3.1.

**PROVISIONAL: every row below comes from the legacy gallery JPEGs only. They are already lossy web JPEGs (about 1 MB each) and often smaller than a tier: WebP 1600 exists on 15 and WebP 2400 on 2 of 24 photos. Their detail is already smoothed, so small tiers compress better than camera originals would and the master row only measures re-encoding a lossy file. Treat byte numbers as a lower bound; put 5 to 10 original JPEGs into samples/originals/ and re-run to get final numbers (plan question 1).**

## Portrait ladder (plan 3.3)

Tiers are chosen by width (variantPlan). Long edge of each tier for a 2:3 portrait master, to check that the portrait detail is not softer than the landscape one:

| Master | WebP tiers (width x height) | JPEG | OG |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3000x2000 | 480x320, 720x480, 960x640, 1600x1067, 2400x1600 | 1600x1067 | 1200x630 |
| 2000x3000 | 480x720, 720x1080, 960x1440, 1600x2400 | 1600x2400 | 1200x630 |
| 2000x2000 | 480x480, 720x720, 960x960, 1600x1600 | 1600x1600 | 1200x630 |
| 1480x987 | 480x320, 720x480, 960x640 | 1480x987 | 1200x630 |

A 2000x3000 portrait master gets its widest WebP tier at 1600x2400: the long edge reaches 2400 px at the 1600 tier, so portrait photos are not softer than landscape ones. A portrait gets the 2400 tier only when its master is at least 2400 px wide (4:5 or squarer at the 3000 px cap); a 2:3 portrait stops at 1600x2400, which is the same 2400 px long edge as the landscape 2400 tier.

## Not measured here

- Real browser canvas output (Chrome/Safari encoders, chroma of canvas JPEG, wide-gamut P3 colour conversion): run `npm run admin-encode-check -- --report` for the Chrome numbers; Safari stays a human VERIFY item.
- Wide-gamut (P3) colour comparison: needs a P3 original; none in the repo.
- AVIF: out of scope (plan 3.4).
